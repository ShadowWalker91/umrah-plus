<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Historical Significance</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The Birth of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ The most significant event associated with this site is the birth of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ , which occurred in the house that once stood here. According to Islamic historical sources, the Prophet ﷺ was born on Monday, the 12th of Rabi' al-Awwal, in the Year of the Elephant, corresponding to approximately April 571 CE. This house belonged to his father, Abdullah ibn Abdul Muttalib, and was located in the Shi'b Banu Hashim, the quarter where the Hashim clan resided in Makkah.</p>

  <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The birth occurred on Monday, 12th of Rabi' al-Awwal according to the majority opinion.</li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The House of Abdullah ibn Abdul Muttalib The original structure on this site was the house of Abdullah ibn Abdul Muttalib, where the Prophet's ﷺ family lived. This house was part of the larger residential area of Shi'b Banu Hashim, where the Hashim clan had their homes. After the Prophet's ﷺ migration to Madinah, the house remained in the possession of his family, specifically his uncle Aqeel ibn Abi Talib, whose descendants later sold it to Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Thaqafi.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Historical Recognition by Scholars and Authorities Throughout Islamic history, this site has been widely recognized by scholars, historians, and Islamic authorities as the authentic birthplace of Prophet Muhammad ﷺClassical historians, including those from the early centuries of Islam, documented this location with consistent chains of narration. In the 20th century, the High Shariah Court of Makkah al-Mukarramah officially affirmed this location through legal deed number 140, dated Jamadi al-Akhirah 1373H (February 1954), confirming its status as the Prophet's ﷺ birthplace.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The Transformation into a Library In the 20th century, the site underwent a significant transformation when the Makkah Al- Mukarramah Library was constructed over the birthplace. This development preserved the location while repurposing it as a center for Islamic learning and scholarship. The library now serves as both a repository of Islamic knowledge and a memorial to the Prophet's ﷺ birth, ensuring that the site remains accessible to pilgrims and scholars while maintaining its dignity and historical significance.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The Demolition and Reconstruction History Historical records indicate that the original structure at this site was demolished and reconstructed on multiple occasions. The first major demolition occurred in 1803, and another reconstruction took place in 1924. Despite these changes, the location itself remained consistently identified as the birthplace of Prophet Muhammad ﷺwith scholars and historians continuing to affirm its authenticity throughout these transformations.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The Site Today Physical Appearance Today The Makkah Al-Mukarramah Library is a modest, well-maintained building with simple architecture. It is designed to function as a library while preserving the dignity of the birthplace site. The structure reflects respect for its historical significance without elaborate decoration. Historical Remains and Structures No original structures from the time of Prophet Muhammad's ﷺ birth remain at this site. The current library is a 20th-century construction on the traditionally identified location where the Prophet ﷺ was born. Modern Development and Surroundings The area around the Makkah Library has developed significantly with Makkah's expansion. It is now located on a main road near Masjid al-Haram, surrounded by modern buildings and infrastructure, yet it remains a distinct landmark. Why Pilgrims Still Visit Pilgrims visit the Makkah Library (Birthplace) to connect with the story of Prophet Muhammad's ﷺ birth and to reflect on the beginning of his earthly life. This site stands as a unique reminder of the moment when the final Messenger of Allah ﷺ entered this world, bringing mercy and guidance to all of humanity. For many visitors, standing at the birthplace of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ inspires a deep sense of gratitude, love, and connection to his mission. It is a place to contemplate the profound significance of his life, the sacrifices he made, and the enduring legacy he left for all Muslims. The site serves as a bridge between the past and present, allowing pilgrims to feel connected to the earliest chapter of Islamic history.
