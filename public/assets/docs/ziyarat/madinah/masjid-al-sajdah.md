<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Masjid al-Sajdah, also known as Masjid Abu Dharr, is one of the historical mosques of Madinah associated with the worship and devotion of the Companions of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. The mosque is remembered particularly because of the event related to Abu Dharr Al-Ghifari (R.A.), a well-known Companion famous for his simplicity, honesty and strong devotion to Islam. The mosque represents the spiritual heritage of Madinah and reflects the places where the early Muslims gathered for prayer and remembrance of Allah.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Historical Background</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Masjid al-Sajdah is linked with a narration involving Abu Dharr Al-Ghifari (R.A.), who was among the early companions of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. The name "Sajdah" is associated with a prostration (Sujood) that took place at this location. Historical accounts mention that this area was among the places connected with the worship practices of the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions. Over time, it became recognised among the historical sites of Madinah.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Islamic Significance</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The mosque holds significance because it reminds visitors of the dedication and humility of the Sahabah. The life of Abu Dharr (R.A.) is an example of sincerity, simplicity and commitment to Islamic teachings. Visiting such historical locations helps Muslims learn about the lives of the Companions and their connection with the Prophet ﷺ and Madinah.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Present Status</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The original historical structure of the mosque has undergone changes over time. The location is still recognised among the historical sites of Madinah and is visited by people interested in exploring Islamic heritage.</p>

<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Interesting Facts</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The mosque is commonly associated with Abu Dharr Al-Ghifari (R.A.). Abu Dharr (R.A.) was among the earliest people to accept Islam. The site reflects the simple lifestyle and devotion of the early Muslim community. It is one of the lesser-known historical locations of Madinah.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Masjid al-Sajdah, commonly known today as Masjid Abu Dharr, is one of the historical mosques associated with the Seerah of Prophet Muhammad ﷺIt is traditionally identified as the place where the Messenger of Allah ﷺ performed a long prostration after receiving glad tidings from Jibril عليه السلام. The mosque is called “al-Sajdah” because of this prostration and “al-Shukr” because it is associated with gratitude to Allah. Its importance comes from the reported event connected with the Prophet ﷺnot from claims of a separate ritual or guaranteed reward for visiting it. For pilgrims exploring Ziyarat in Madinah, the mosque offers an opportunity to learn about gratitude, prayer, and the careful preservation of places linked with Islamic history. Its story is remembered through historical works and modern heritage records, although the strength of the report describing the event should be understood honestly.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The Report of the Prostration The event associated with Masjid al-Sajdah was narrated by Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf رضي الله عنهHe reported seeing the Prophet ﷺ enter a palm garden and remain in prostration for so long that he feared the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had passed away. When the Prophet ﷺ raised his head, he explained that Jibril عليه السلام  had brought glad tidings concerning blessings and greetings for those who sent blessings and peace upon him. The Prophet ﷺ then prostrated in gratitude to Allah.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Names Connected With the Place Historical sources record several names for the site. These names reflect its location, its surrounding landscape, or the event associated with it. “Masjid al- Sajdah” refers to the prostration, while “Masjid al-Shukr” refers to gratitude. The name “Masjid Abu Dharr” became widely used in later centuries, although the mosque is not being presented as a place where Abu Dharr رضي الله عنه necessarily lived or performed a uniquely established act of worship.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Historical Evidence and Caution The identification of the present mosque with the place of the Prophet’s prostration comes through historical and local tradition recorded by Madinah historians. The Madinah Heritage Encyclopedia cites early and later works, including accounts by al-Mutri and al-Samhudi. However, the narration used for the event has been judged weak by a hadith editor. Therefore, the story may be shared as a reported historical association, but it should not be stated as an unquestionable authentic hadith.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The Mosque Through the Centuries The physical building did not always resemble the mosque visitors see today. Historical descriptions portray an earlier, very small structure, and later writers recorded periods when it was damaged or partly ruined. Its continued identification reflects Madinah’s long effort to preserve places connected with the Prophetic period, even when buildings changed or disappeared.</p>

<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">SITE TODAY</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">A Preserved Historical Mosque Today, Masjid al-Sajdah remains a functioning historical mosque in northern Madinah. Its present identity comes from the combination of its traditional names, its recorded association with the Prophet ﷺand its continued place within Madinah’s heritage landscape. Modern Architectural Form The mosque has been rebuilt in a modern architectural form. Heritage records state that its area was expanded to approximately 182.3 square metres, with a minaret added at the northwestern corner. Visitors should remember that the current structure is a later building marking a historically identified location, not the unchanged original structure. Reaching the Site Its proximity to Al-Masjid an-Nabawi makes it accessible during a visit to central Madinah. Visitors should confirm the route locally, allow time for traffic and walking, and follow current directions because streets and access arrangements can change. Conduct During a Visit The mosque should be approached as a place of worship and heritage, not as a site for invented rituals. Visitors may pray when permitted, learn its history, and maintain quiet, cleanliness, and respect. No special prayer, guaranteed reward, or independent religious virtue should be attributed to the mosque without reliable evidence.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">WHY IT STILL MATTERS</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Masjid al-Sajdah matters because its remembered history places gratitude at the centre of a human moment. The report does not describe worldly power or victory; it describes a servant of Allah ﷺ responding to good news through prostration. That detail gives the site a quiet lesson: blessings are not only received with words, but also acknowledged through humility before Allah. At the same time, differing assessments of the report teach visitors to respect both devotion and historical responsibility. A meaningful Ziyarat in Madinah allows the heart to remember the Seerah while the mind remains careful about what can truly be claimed.</p>
