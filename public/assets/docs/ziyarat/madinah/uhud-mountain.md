<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Jabal Uhud is one of the most famous and historically significant mountains in Madinah. It is remembered as the location of the Battle of Uhud, one of the major battles between the Muslims and the Quraysh of Makkah. The mountain holds a special place in Islamic history because of its connection with Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and the sacrifices of the Companions (Sahabah), especially the martyrs of Uhud.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Historical Background</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The Battle of Uhud took place in 3 AH (625 CE) near the northern side of Madinah. The Quraysh army came to take revenge after their defeat at the Battle of Badr. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and the Muslim army confronted them near Mount Uhud. During the battle, many companions displayed great courage and sacrifice. The famous archers' position on a nearby hill became an important strategic point. Due to changes during the battle, many companions were martyred, including Hamza ibn Abdul Muttalib (R.A.), the uncle of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Islamic Significance</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Jabal Uhud is significant because it reminds Muslims of the sacrifices made by the early believers. The Battle of Uhud teaches important lessons about obedience, patience, unity, and trust in Allah. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ showed great love and respect for this mountain. It is reported that he said:</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">"Uhud is a mountain that loves us and we love it."</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">This narration reflects the special connection between the Prophet ﷺ and Mount Uhud.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Present Status</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Today, Jabal Uhud remains one of the most visited historical sites in Madinah. The area has been developed to facilitate visitors while preserving its historical importance. Near the mountain is the Maqbarah Shuhada Uhud (Cemetery of Uhud Martyrs), where many companions who were martyred in the battle are remembered.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Location</h3>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Name: Jabal Uhud (Uhud Mountain)</h3>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">City: Madinah</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Area: Northern Madinah, approximately 4–5 km from Masjid an-Nabawi. The mountain extends across a large area and is easily accessible from the central area of Madinah.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Visitor Information</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Open for visitors throughout the year. Usually included in Madinah historical Ziyarat tours. Visitors should maintain respect due to the historical and religious importance of the site. No specific worship rituals are prescribed at the mountain. Visitors often combine this visit with the Cemetery of Uhud Martyrs and nearby historical locations.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Interesting Facts</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Jabal Uhud is one of the most well-known mountains mentioned in Islamic history. The Battle of Uhud occurred during the lifetime of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Many prominent companions were martyred here. The mountain consists of red volcanic rocks and has a unique geographical appearance. It remains a symbol of faith, sacrifice, and perseverance.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Uhud Mountain is one of the most important places in Madinah Ziyarat. It is not only a mountain to look at. It is a place that carries a great part of Islamic memory, because the “Battle of Uhud” took place near it and shaped the history of the early Muslim community. For many visitors, Uhud feels different from other historical places in Madinah. It is quiet, wide, and deeply emotional. The mountain stands with a kind of dignity, and the history tied to it is not light or distant. It brings the reader close to a day when the Muslims faced hardship, loss, courage, and obedience all at once. It also reminds visitors that Islamic history was lived through real human struggle, not only words on a page. Uhud is also remembered with love because of the Messenger of Allah ﷺAuthentic reports describe his affection for the mountain, and that gives the place a special warmth in the heart of Madinah. When visitors come here, they are not only seeing a landmark. They are standing near one of the clearest reminders of sacrifice, patience, and faith in the Seerah.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Uhud in the Seerah Uhud became part of Islamic history in the third year after the Hijrah, when the Muslims of Madinah met the Quraysh in battle near the mountain. The event is remembered not only as a military encounter, but as a turning point in the Seerah. It showed the strength of the Muslim community, but it also showed how one moment of disobedience could lead to pain, loss, and deep reflection.</p>

<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">SITE TODAY</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Present-Day Setting Today, Uhud Mountain stands as an open natural landmark north of Madinah. Its long shape, reddish stone, and wide presence make it easy to recognize. The mountain does not need decoration or explanation to feel important. Its weight comes from the history attached to it, and that history is still felt by many visitors who come with love and respect. Visitor Experience Most visitors come to see the mountain itself, the battlefield area, and nearby places connected with the “Battle of Uhud.” The visit is usually calm and reflective. People do not come here for entertainment. They come to remember the sacrifice of the Companions رضي الله عنهمto think about the Seerah, and to feel closer to the early history of Islam. Physical Appearance Uhud is a rugged mountain with a distinctive line and natural slope across the northern side of Madinah. Its reddish rock and broad form give it a strong and steady look. The landscape around it is open and simple, which helps the visitor focus on the meaning of the place rather than on anything decorative or distracting. Respectful Conduct Because the site is tied to martyrdom and to the Seerah, visitors are expected to approach it with calmness and respect. It is not just a place to take photos or pass through quickly. It is one of the places to visit in Madinah where remembrance matters. A quiet attitude, simple manners, and respectful conduct fit the spirit of the site.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">WHY IT STILL MATTERS</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Uhud still matters because it speaks to something every generation needs to remember: faith is tested in real life, not in easy moments. The mountain carries the memory of courage, but also the pain that came when instruction was not fully followed. That makes it a powerful place for reflection. It teaches that trust, patience, and responsibility are not small matters in the life of a believer. It also matters because it keeps the lives of the Companions رضي الله عنهم close to us. These were real people who stood beside the Prophet Muhammad ﷺstruggled, suffered, and gave their lives in one of the most difficult moments in Madinah’s early history. Their sacrifice gives the site its weight. Visitors do not come here to admire stone alone. They come to remember people, choices, and the price of holding firm when the moment became hard. For many Muslims, Uhud is one of the places where Islamic history feels especially human. It is not distant, and it is not abstract. It is a mountain that remembers loss, loyalty, and love for the Messenger of Allah ﷺThat is why Uhud Mountain remains one of the most meaningful sites in Madinah Ziyarat.</p>
