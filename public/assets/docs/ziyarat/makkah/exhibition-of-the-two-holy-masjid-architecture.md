<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">connects visitors with the stories of sacrifice, faith, and service that have shaped these sacred spaces over more than fourteen centuries. Historical Significance Origins and Purpose of the Exhibition The Two Holy Mosques Exhibition was established to document and celebrate the architectural evolution and spiritual significance of Masjid al-Haram and Masjid al-Nabawi. Its creation reflects a broader effort by the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia to preserve Islamic heritage and make it accessible to pilgrims, scholars, and visitors from around the world. The exhibition was inaugurated in 2000 as part of ongoing initiatives to enrich the experience of Hajj and Umrah pilgrims. It serves not only as a museum but also as an educational resource, offering insights into the historical, artistic, and religious dimensions of the Two Holy Mosques. Architectural Evolution of the Haramain One of the central themes of the exhibition is the architectural transformation of the Grand Mosque and the Prophet's Mosque across different historical periods. Visitors can explore:</p>
<ul style="list-style-type: disc; margin-left: 20px; color: #d1d5db; margin-bottom: 16px;">
  <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Models and diagrams showing the expansion and renovation of both mosques over time.</li>
  <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Historical photographs documenting changes in structure, design, and capacity.</li>
  <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Information about the materials, techniques, and craftsmanship used in various eras.</li>
  <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The role of different Islamic caliphates and kingdoms in maintaining and enhancing the sacred spaces.</li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">This journey through architectural history helps visitors appreciate the scale of effort and devotion invested in caring for the places where millions of Muslims pray every day. Sacred Artifacts and Manuscripts The exhibition houses a remarkable collection of sacred objects and historical items, including:</p>
<ul style="list-style-type: disc; margin-left: 20px; color: #d1d5db; margin-bottom: 16px;">
  <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Rare manuscripts and copies of the Noble Qur'an retrieved from the libraries of the</li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Grand Mosque and the Prophet's Mosque.</p>
<ul style="list-style-type: disc; margin-left: 20px; color: #d1d5db; margin-bottom: 16px;">
  <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Ancient photographs and documents related to the Haramain.</li>
  <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Six marble tiles engraved with the word "Allah", the name of the Prophet Muhammad</li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">ﷺand the names of the Four Rightly Guided Caliphs (RA), dating back to 1299 AH.</p>
<ul style="list-style-type: disc; margin-left: 20px; color: #d1d5db; margin-bottom: 16px;">
  <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Items connected with the Kaaba, including historical elements from its structure and decoration.</li>
</ul>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">These artifacts provide a tangible connection to the spiritual and intellectual heritage of Islam, allowing visitors to reflect on the continuity of faith and knowledge across generations. The Role of the Kiswa and Sacred Services Located near the Kiswa Factory, the exhibition also highlights the honoured tradition of producing and maintaining the Kiswa, the sacred cloth that covers the Kaaba. This connection underscores the deep care and reverence with which the Haramain have been served throughout history. The exhibition sheds light on:</p>
<ul style="list-style-type: disc; margin-left: 20px; color: #d1d5db; margin-bottom: 16px;">
  <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The history of the Kiswa and its symbolic importance.</li>
  <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The craftsmanship involved in its creation.</li>
  <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The broader network of services dedicated to the care of the Two Holy Mosques, including water supply, lighting, cleaning, and security.</li>
</ul>

<ul style="list-style-type: disc; margin-left: 20px; color: #d1d5db; margin-bottom: 16px;">

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">A Journey Through Time and Devotion Walking through the Two Holy Mosques Exhibition feels like stepping into a living timeline of Islamic history. Each artifact, photograph, and model tells a story of faith, sacrifice, and service. Visitors can trace the journey of the Haramain from their humble beginnings to their current status as centres of global Islamic worship. The exhibition invites reflection on:</p>
<ul style="list-style-type: disc; margin-left: 20px; color: #d1d5db; margin-bottom: 16px;">

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">While visiting the exhibition is not an act of worship in itself, it enriches the pilgrim's experience by connecting them with the historical and cultural dimensions of the Haramain. Frequently Asked Questions Where is the Two Holy Mosques Exhibition located in Makkah? It is located in the Um al-Joud area, near the Kiswa Factory, approximately 10 kilometres from Masjid al-Haram. What can visitors see at the Two Holy Mosques Exhibition? Visitors can see rare artifacts, ancient manuscripts, historical photographs, architectural models, and sacred objects connected with the Grand Mosque and the Prophet's Mosque. Is the Two Holy Mosques Exhibition open every day? No, it is open Sunday to Thursday from 8:00 AM to 8:00 PM and is closed on Friday and Saturday; it may also close briefly during prayer times.</p>
