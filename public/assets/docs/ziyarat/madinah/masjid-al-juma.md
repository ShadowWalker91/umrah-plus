<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Masjid al-Juma, also known as Masjid Bani Salim, is one of the most significant historical mosques in Madinah. It marks the location where Prophet Muhammad ﷺ led the first Jumu'ah (Friday) prayer after his migration (Hijrah) from Makkah to Madinah.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Historical Background</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">After leaving Quba, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ continued his journey towards Madinah. Upon reaching the valley of Ranuna, the time for Friday prayer arrived. The Prophet ﷺ led the first Jumu'ah prayer at this location, attended by many companions. A mosque was later built to commemorate this historic event, becoming one of the most important landmarks in Madinah.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Islamic Significance</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Masjid al-Juma commemorates the establishment of the first Friday congregational prayer in Islamic history after the Hijrah. It symbolizes the beginning of organised Muslim community life in Madinah and reflects the importance of Jumu'ah in Islam.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Present Status</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The mosque has been renovated several times while preserving its historical significance. Today, it is maintained by the Saudi authorities and remains open for worshippers and visitors.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Location</h3>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Name: Masjid al-Juma (Masjid Bani Salim)</h3>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">City: Madinah</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Area: Wadi Ranuna, between Masjid Quba and Masjid an-Nabawi.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Visitor Information</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Open daily for prayers. Frequently visited by pilgrims travelling between Quba and Masjid an-Nabawi. Modest Islamic dress is required. Easily accessible by road.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Interesting Facts</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Site of the first Friday prayer after Hijrah. Also known as Masjid Bani Salim. Located on the historic route taken by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ during the Hijrah. One of Madinah's most visited historical mosques.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Masjid al-Juma’ is one of the most meaningful mosques in Madinah because it is remembered as the place where the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ led the first Friday prayer after the Hijrah. That moment gives the mosque a special place in Islamic history. It was not a large political gathering or a grand public ceremony. It was a prayer, a khutbah, and a community standing together in a new chapter of their lives. For visitors, the mosque carries a quiet but powerful feeling. It belongs to the earliest days of Madinah, when the Muhajirun had left Makkah and were settling into a new home, and the Ansar were welcoming them with sincerity and care. In that setting, the first Jumu’ah prayer became more than a weekly obligation. It became a sign that the new Muslim community had begun to take shape in Madinah with worship at its center.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The first Jumu’ah in Madinah Masjid al-Juma’ is known as the place where Prophet Muhammad ﷺ led the first Friday prayer after the Hijrah. This is its most important historical meaning. Friday prayer in Islam is not just another prayer time; it is the weekly congregation of the Muslim community, with the khutbah reminding people of faith, obedience, and unity. At this site, that practice was established in the new city of Madinah. The moment was important because the Muslim community was no longer only a group in migration. It was becoming a settled ummah with shared worship and shared direction. The first Jumu’ah prayer carried both spiritual and social meaning. It showed that Madinah was now a city where the Muslim community could gather openly and worship together under the guidance of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The Hijrah setting The mosque is linked to the period when the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ had just arrived in the Madinah region after leaving Makkah. The Hijrah was a major turning point in Islamic history. It was not only a journey of distance; it was a journey of faith, sacrifice, and new beginnings. Masjid al-Juma’ reflects that atmosphere very clearly because it is tied to one of the first public acts of worship after that migration. The setting matters because it shows how Islam grew through both movement and settlement. The early Muslims had left homes, possessions, and familiar surroundings behind. In Madinah, they began again with prayer and brotherhood. The first Jumu’ah was therefore a sign of stability after hardship, and of unity after separation. That is part of what makes the mosque feel emotionally strong even today.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The role of the Ansar The “Ansar” were the Muslims of Madinah who welcomed and supported the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and the Muhajirun. Their generosity was part of what made Madinah a place of faith and safety. Masjid al-Juma’ stands within that larger story because the first Friday prayer was not only about a new location. It was also about a community learning to live together in loyalty, mercy, and shared worship. This makes the site feel deeply human. The mosque is tied to a moment when support, migration, and prayer came together. The Ansar did not simply offer shelter; they helped build a society. That is why the mosque is not only historical. It is a reminder of the character of Madinah itself, and of the bonds that shaped the early Muslim ummah.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Preservation and continuity Masjid al-Juma’ was later rebuilt and maintained so that its memory would remain visible. That preservation matters because it keeps the first Jumu’ah site alive for later generations. Madinah has grown and changed, but the mosque continues to point visitors back to the early days of Islam. Its value is not only in the building itself, but in the history it carries. Over time, the mosque became one of the known places to visit in Madinah for those who want to understand the Seerah more deeply. Its survival shows that the Muslim community has continued to honor places connected with the Prophet Muhammad ﷺIn that sense, the mosque is part of Madinah’s living memory, not a distant relic.</p>

<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">SITE TODAY</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Present-day identity Today, Masjid al-Juma’ is a historical mosque visited by those exploring Islamic heritage in Madinah. It is not a large monument, but it holds a respected place because of what happened there. The mosque’s identity comes from worship and memory, and that gives it a calm dignity. Physical setting The mosque stands in an area associated with the old route between Quba and Madinah, near Wadi Ranuna. Its surroundings remind visitors that the early Muslim community lived and prayed in real landscapes, not abstract settings. The area feels connected to both movement and stillness. Visitor experience Many visitors come here as part of a wider Madinah Ziyarat route. The experience is often quiet and reflective. People usually pause to remember the first Jumu’ah, the Hijrah, and the meaning of worship at the beginning of a new chapter in Islamic history.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">WHY IT STILL MATTERS</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Masjid al-Juma’ still matters because it reminds us that Islam’s great history was built through worship as much as through events. The first Jumu’ah prayer in Madinah was not a spectacle. It was a faithful community gathering around the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in a new place, after sacrifice and migration. That makes the mosque deeply moving. For modern visitors, the site teaches that sacred history is lived, not only remembered. It shows the beauty of the Hijrah, the strength of the Ansar, and the beginning of a community rooted in prayer. In Madinah Ziyarat, it is one of those places that quietly helps the heart understand the Seerah better.</p>
