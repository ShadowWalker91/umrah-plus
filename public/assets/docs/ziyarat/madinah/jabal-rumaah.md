<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Jabal Rumaah, also known as the Mountain of Archers, is one of the most important historical landmarks near Mount Uhud in Madinah. It is famous because of its connection with the Battle of Uhud, one of the major battles in early Islamic history. The mountain gained its name because Prophet Muhammad ﷺ positioned a group of archers there to protect the Muslim army during the battle.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Historical Background</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">During the Battle of Uhud in 3 AH (625 CE), Prophet Muhammad ﷺ appointed approximately fifty archers under the leadership of Abdullah ibn Jubair (R.A.) on this small hill. Their responsibility was to protect the Muslim army from an attack from behind. Initially, the Muslims gained advantage in the battle. However, when some archers left their position thinking the battle had ended, the opposing forces attacked from that side. This event became a significant lesson regarding obedience, discipline and following the instructions of leadership.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Islamic Significance</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Jabal Rumaah holds great importance because it is directly connected with the lessons of the Battle of Uhud. The event teaches Muslims the importance of patience, discipline, obedience and trust in Allah. The location reminds visitors about the sacrifices made by the Sahabah (R.A.) and the challenges faced by the early Muslim community.</p>
<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Present Status</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Jabal Rumaah remains a preserved historical landmark near Mount Uhud. Visitors from around the world visit this area to learn about the events of the Battle of Uhud and remember the sacrifices of the Companions.</p>

<h3 style="margin-top: 24px; margin-bottom: 12px; color: #F9C344;">Interesting Facts</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">The mountain received its name because archers were stationed there during the Battle of Uhud. It represents one of the most important strategic points in early Islamic military history. The Battle of Uhud occurred in the third year after Hijrah. The event is frequently discussed in Islamic history due to the lessons it provides</p>
<p style="text-align: justify; margin-bottom: 12px; line-height: 1.7;">Jabal al-Rumah, also known as Jabal al-Rumat and Jabal Aynayn, is a small hill that carries a very large place in Islamic history. It was here that the Noble Prophet ﷺ placed the archers to protect the Muslim rear during the "Battle of Uhud," and that one decision became part of a lesson Muslims remember with both sorrow and respect. The hill itself is simple, but the meaning attached to it is deep: courage was present there, obedience was tested there, and the pain of a difficult turning point was felt there. For visitors, this is not just another stop in Madinah Ziyarat. It is a place that invites silence, reflection, and a humble heart. The ground near Uhud reminds a person that hardship can come quickly, even after hope, and that the Seerah is not only a story of victory but also of patience, sacrifice, and responsibility. It is the kind of place that does not need to speak loudly to leave an impression. The human weight of Jabal al-Rumah lies in the people who stood there under clear instructions, aware that their position mattered. When the archers left their posts, the battle changed, and that change became one of the most powerful lessons in Islamic history about trust, discipline, and the heavy cost of disobedience. That memory still feels alive because it reflects a real moment of struggle, not a distant legend. Historical Background The Site in the Life of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ The hill became important during the planning for the forces at Uhud. It was chosen because the Muslim army needed protection from the rear, especially against cavalry movement around the battlefield. That practical decision gave Jabal al-Rumah a lasting place in Islamic history and in the wider story of Madinah.</p>
